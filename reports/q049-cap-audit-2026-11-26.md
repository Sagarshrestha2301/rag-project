# Audit: what the two-extra-page cap excluded on q049 (2026-11-26)

**Question asked by the owner:** the cap in step 18 dropped q049 in a number of older runs; is it
hiding a real citation problem?

**Answer:** No — and the audit found two things that matter more than the question.

1. **The count in the record is wrong.** It is **ten** runs, not six. The step-18 report, the
   `STATUS.md` rows and the `EVAL.md` row all say six; ten stored runs carry a q049 answer with
   three extra citations, and all ten lose q049 to the cap.
2. **The cap is excluding a correct, prompt-compliant answer, not a bad citation.** Its unit is
   wrong: the risk it exists to stop is an answer built from an *unrelated document*, and the shape
   it actually catches is an answer that cites several pages of *the right paper*, which is exactly
   what the `s6-3` prompt rule (D-071) demands.

## Evidence

q049: *"If a household has no elderly members, what age qualifies a member to plant bamboo?"* Gold
is p. 5 of the Das 1970 bamboo paper, with the quote about "40 years or over".

Ten runs have a four-citation answer, in two distinct citation sets. **Every quotation in both sets
is verbatim on the page it names** (checked with `corpus.page_text`, the shipped reader), and **all
four pages are in the same document** — no distractor paper, no invented page, no paraphrase:

| page | gold? | quotation | sentence it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| p. 5 | **gold** | "In the absence of old age members in the family it can be carried out by the middle aged household heads or members (40 years or over)." | the 40-years rule |
| p. 4 | extra | "If there are no old males (over 60 years) in the house then it can be carried out by a middle-aged man." | the same rule, stated on the previous page |
| p. 6 | extra | "…general belief among the households that bamboos should not be planted… only by the oldest household male members" | "the belief that only the oldest male should plant" |
| p. 3 | extra | "Some of the households mentioned that women can carry out bamboo planting if they already…" | "some households allow women to plant" |

The answer's own markers map one-to-one onto those pages (`[2]` → p. 5 for the gold fact, `[3]` →
p. 6, `[4]` → p. 3, `[1]` → p. 4), so this is the prompt's "each sentence quotes its own evidence"
rule being followed, not padding. A reader who followed the citations would find supporting text on
every page.

## Why the cap caught it anyway

Step 18 counts **pages** outside the gold set and allows two. Four sentences wanting four pages of
one paper is three extras, so the answer fails a rule aimed at unrelated sources. Measured across
the current runs:

- **Answers citing an extra document: 1 in every run** (q003, the 1975 rainfall paper), **maximum
  1**. No answer anywhere in the stored history cites two other documents.
- **Extra pages within the right document** go up to 3 (q044, q045, q049). q044 and q045 fail for
  an unrelated reason — their quotations are paraphrases, so they fail under any cap.

So the discriminating quantity is **extra documents**, not extra pages. A cap of two extra
*documents* would keep every current result, keep q049 in all ten historical runs, keep q003 (one
extra document, a real supporting citation), and still fail an answer built from distractors.

## Today's impact: zero

Every run from 2026-11-22 onward cites one or two extras on q049 and passes under the current cap.
`2026-11-25-step17-list-shape` (43/55) and `2026-11-24-repro-a` (40/55) are unaffected. The cap
costs only the historical runs, which is why the finding changes no current number — but it does
mean the step-18 "cost" I recorded was mis-described: it is not padding being caught.

## Recommendation

**Change the cap's unit, not its size** (PROPOSED D-099): keep "at least one gold page", keep "every
quotation verbatim", and count **cited documents outside the gold set**, allowing two. Record both
counts (`extra_citations` for pages, plus `extra_documents`). If approved, re-score the stored runs
again — q049 returns in the ten older runs and nothing else moves, which the step-18 measurement
already tells us because no other answer exceeds one extra document.

If the owner prefers not to change the rule again, the alternative is to accept the loss and say so
plainly: the cap costs ten historical q049 answers that are correct and fully cited, and it buys a
limit on pages that no current answer reaches.

## Not measured

- Whether four-page answers appear in future runs: the model's citation density fell after the
  `s6-3` prompt (later runs use one or two extras), so the historical shape may simply not recur.
- Whether a two-*document* cap is the right number: no stored answer cites even two other
  documents, so any value from 1 upward would behave identically on this history.
